# DermaVue literature review matrix

Review date: 2026-10-01  
Search scope: skin-lesion classification, multimodal fusion, image quality,
reliability, selective prediction, domain shift, and reporting guidance.

This is a focused scoping review for protocol design, not a claim of
systematic-review completeness. Bibliographic details were checked against the
linked publisher, repository, or official dataset page. Peer-reviewed work and
preprints are labeled separately.

## Preliminary conclusions

1. A simple “multimodal fusion” or “attention fusion” contribution is not
   defensibly novel: Derm7pt work and later attention-fusion studies already
   establish that direction.
2. Image-quality assessment, abstention, calibration, and domain generalization
   are also established areas. The plausible contribution is a bounded empirical
   framework that combines quality indicators and genuine cross-modal
   disagreement to study selective risk, with source-separated testing.
3. That framework is only feasible if genuinely paired clinical/dermoscopic
   cases with stable lesion/patient IDs exist. Unpaired datasets must not be
   joined by filename, diagnosis, or visual similarity.
4. The current post-freeze binary analysis is exploratory rather than a novel
   method: at 50% confidence coverage, selective error was low while retained
   melanoma sensitivity was also low. This supports sensitivity-aware reporting
   of selective prediction, not a claim of algorithmic novelty.
5. No novelty claim can be made until the final method, comparator set, and
   dataset are fixed and a broader database search is completed.
6. ISIC 2020 is a public external-evaluation candidate, not evidence of
   independent validation yet. MRA-MIDAS remains the preferred paired-data
   source but requires legitimate access authorization.
